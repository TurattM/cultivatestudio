In one minute

A good kitchen produces a lot of knowledge: recipes, prices, menus, ideas, the people who make them. Most of it lives in folders, notebooks and spreadsheets that nobody can find when the chef is not there.

Cultivate Studio keeps all of it in one working place. A dish is developed, approved, costed and put on a menu without ever being retyped. The food cost is always current. Every outlet sees its own numbers. A new chef opens the app and finds the kitchen exactly as it was left.

The recipe is in a Word file on someone’s laptop.Every recipe is in one library, with photo, method, allergens and cost, open from any phone.
Food cost is calculated once, at menu launch, then forgotten.Prices come from your purchasing list; when a price moves, every dish that uses it updates itself.
New dishes are tested in a notebook and lost when the sous chef leaves.Every version, tasting note and photo is kept; the chef approves with one tap and the recipe is born costed.
Seven outlets, seven ways of doing things.One central kitchen, every outlet with its own room, recipes and menus shared between them in a click.

Ask Cultivate

What is in season in Southern Thailand in October, and what pairs with torch ginger?

October: mangosteen, rambutan, sator, pak wan pa and the first wild pepper. Torch ginger sits well with acid and sea — try it with your kung tamari, lime leaf and raw scallop. Your plum ferment would echo its sourness.

Why a chef built it

Marco Turatti has spent sixteen years opening and leading luxury hotel kitchens across Europe, the Middle East and Asia, from Michelin-starred dining rooms to a resort with seven outlets, a farm and a fermentation lab. Cultivate Studio is the system he built, alone, to run them — and it is used in his kitchens every day.
